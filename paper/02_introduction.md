# I. Introduction

The rapid expansion of Internet of Things (IoT) technologies and cloud computing infrastructures has transformed the operational landscape of modern digital ecosystems. Smart cities, industrial automation platforms, healthcare monitoring systems, intelligent transportation networks, and critical infrastructure environments increasingly rely on interconnected IoT devices that continuously exchange data through cloud-enabled services. While this integration has significantly improved scalability, efficiency, and service availability, it has also introduced a substantially larger and more complex cyberattack surface [13], [14].

Recent years have witnessed a significant increase in sophisticated cyber threats targeting IoT-cloud environments. Attackers frequently exploit vulnerabilities in connected devices, communication protocols, cloud services, and identity management mechanisms to launch attacks such as distributed denial-of-service (DDoS), credential theft, malware propagation, unauthorized access, replay attacks, and botnet command-and-control operations [11], [15], [17]. The heterogeneous nature of IoT deployments, combined with the massive volume of generated telemetry data, makes real-time detection and response increasingly challenging for traditional security architectures.

Conventional cybersecurity systems primarily focus on isolated intrusion detection mechanisms or centralized security monitoring platforms. Although machine learning-based intrusion detection systems have demonstrated promising performance in identifying malicious activities [14], [16], most existing approaches operate as standalone detection models and provide limited support for subsequent stages of cyber defense operations. In many practical environments, security analysts must manually interpret alerts, investigate attack characteristics, determine appropriate mitigation strategies, and preserve forensic evidence for future investigations. Such manual workflows often introduce delays that reduce the effectiveness of incident response and increase organizational exposure to cyber threats.

The emergence of multi-agent artificial intelligence systems provides new opportunities for developing autonomous cybersecurity architectures capable of distributing specialized tasks among collaborative intelligent agents [1], [4], [5]. Rather than relying on a single monolithic model, multi-agent systems enable different agents to perform dedicated functions such as attack detection, threat analysis, response generation, trust evaluation, and decision support. This modular design improves scalability, interpretability, and adaptability in complex cyber defense scenarios [6].

Despite recent advances in AI-driven cybersecurity, several important challenges remain insufficiently addressed. First, many existing solutions operate as black-box systems and provide limited explanation regarding the reasoning behind security decisions [20]–[24]. Second, autonomous cybersecurity systems often lack mechanisms for evaluating the trustworthiness of generated recommendations before executing defensive actions [34]–[40]. Third, incident logs are commonly stored in centralized repositories that may be modified, deleted, or manipulated by attackers, thereby compromising forensic investigations and auditability [25]–[33]. These limitations highlight the need for an integrated framework capable of combining intelligent threat detection, explainable decision support, trust-aware automation, and tamper-resistant evidence preservation.

To address these challenges, this paper proposes a Multi-Agent AI Cyber Defense Framework for IoT-cloud environments. The proposed architecture integrates six specialized components: a Detection Agent for identifying malicious activities, a Threat Analysis Agent for attack classification, an Explainability Agent for generating interpretable threat intelligence, a Response Agent for recommending mitigation actions, a Trust Manager for evaluating confidence-based decision reliability, and a Blockchain Logger for maintaining an immutable incident evidence ledger. Unlike traditional intrusion detection systems that focus solely on attack identification, the proposed framework provides an end-to-end autonomous cyber defense workflow that supports detection, analysis, explanation, response, trust assessment, and forensic logging within a unified architecture.

The proposed framework is evaluated using the IoT-Cloud-Sec dataset [41], a large-scale cybersecurity dataset containing 100,000 IoT-cloud events across multiple attack categories. Experimental results demonstrate that the framework achieves a detection rate of 93.0%, a threat classification accuracy of 91.8%, and successful blockchain-based preservation of incident evidence while maintaining trust-aware decision support throughout the cyber defense process.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows. First, a multi-agent cybersecurity architecture is developed to support collaborative and autonomous cyber defense operations in IoT-cloud environments. Second, a trust-aware decision management mechanism is introduced to evaluate the reliability of AI-generated security recommendations. Third, an explainable threat intelligence component is incorporated to improve the transparency and interpretability of security decisions. Fourth, a blockchain-based incident logging mechanism is designed to ensure tamper-resistant storage of forensic evidence and response records. Finally, the effectiveness of the proposed framework is validated through extensive experimentation using a large-scale IoT-cloud cybersecurity dataset.
