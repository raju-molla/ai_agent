# VI. Conclusion and Future Work

This paper presented a Multi-Agent AI Cyber Defense Framework for securing IoT-cloud environments through the integration of artificial intelligence, explainable threat intelligence, trust-aware decision support, automated response generation, and blockchain-based forensic logging. The proposed architecture was designed to address several limitations of conventional cybersecurity systems, including the lack of explainability, insufficient trust evaluation mechanisms, fragmented security workflows, and vulnerability of centralized logging infrastructures.

The framework incorporates six specialized components that collaboratively perform cyber defense operations. The Detection Agent identifies malicious activities from IoT-cloud telemetry data, while the Threat Analysis Agent performs hierarchical attack classification to determine the specific threat category. The Explainability Agent enhances transparency by providing interpretable explanations for AI-generated decisions. The Response Agent generates context-aware mitigation recommendations, and the Trust Manager evaluates the reliability of security decisions before determining the appropriate level of automation. Finally, the Blockchain Logger preserves all incident evidence in an immutable ledger to ensure forensic integrity and auditability.

Experimental evaluation was conducted using the IoT-Cloud-Sec dataset consisting of 100,000 IoT-cloud events and multiple cyberattack categories. The Detection Agent achieved a classification accuracy of 98.54% with an F1-score of 95.90%, while the Threat Analysis Agent obtained a threat classification accuracy of 91.8%. End-to-end evaluation of the complete framework demonstrated a detection rate of 93.0%, an average trust score of 0.716, and successful blockchain integrity verification across all recorded incidents. These results indicate that the proposed framework can effectively combine intelligent threat detection, attack analysis, explainable decision support, trust-aware automation, and immutable evidence preservation within a unified cybersecurity architecture.

The findings further demonstrate that trust-aware decision management can serve as an important safeguard for autonomous cybersecurity systems by balancing automation and human oversight. In addition, blockchain-based incident logging provides a reliable mechanism for maintaining tamper-resistant security records, thereby supporting forensic investigations, compliance requirements, and long-term auditability. The Explainability Agent also contributes to improving analyst confidence by providing interpretable justifications for security decisions generated by machine learning models.

Despite the promising results, several limitations remain. The current implementation relies on supervised machine learning models and offline evaluation using historical cybersecurity data. Consequently, model performance may be influenced by the characteristics of the training dataset and may require periodic retraining to adapt to evolving threat landscapes. Furthermore, the blockchain component currently employs a lightweight hash-chaining mechanism rather than a fully decentralized blockchain network.

Future research will focus on extending the framework through the integration of large language model (LLM)-based autonomous security agents, federated threat intelligence sharing, adaptive trust management mechanisms, and real-time streaming analytics. Additional investigation will also explore the deployment of smart contracts and enterprise blockchain platforms, such as Hyperledger Fabric and Ethereum, to support decentralized incident management and collaborative cyber defense. Finally, future versions of the framework will incorporate advanced explainability techniques, including SHAP-based feature attribution and counterfactual explanations, to further improve transparency and analyst trust.

In summary, the proposed Multi-Agent AI Cyber Defense Framework demonstrates that the combination of intelligent agent collaboration, explainable threat intelligence, trust-aware automation, and blockchain-backed accountability represents a promising direction for the development of next-generation autonomous cybersecurity systems capable of protecting increasingly complex IoT-cloud infrastructures.
