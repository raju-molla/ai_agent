# Abstract

The rapid proliferation of Internet of Things (IoT) devices and cloud-integrated services has significantly expanded the cyberattack surface of modern digital infrastructures. Traditional cybersecurity solutions often rely on isolated detection mechanisms and centralized incident management approaches, which are increasingly challenged by the scale, heterogeneity, and dynamic nature of contemporary IoT-cloud environments. Moreover, the lack of transparency in artificial intelligence (AI)-driven security decisions and the vulnerability of conventional logging systems to tampering further complicate effective cyber defense operations.

This paper presents a Multi-Agent AI Cyber Defense Framework that integrates attack detection, threat analysis, explainable threat intelligence, trust-aware decision making, automated response generation, and blockchain-based incident logging within a unified architecture. The proposed framework employs a Detection Agent to identify malicious activities, a Threat Analysis Agent to classify attack categories, an Explainability Agent to provide interpretable security insights, a Response Agent to recommend mitigation actions, and a Trust Manager to evaluate the reliability of autonomous decisions. To ensure forensic integrity and auditability, all incident evidence and response records are stored in an immutable blockchain-based ledger.

The framework is evaluated using the IoT-Cloud-Sec dataset, a large-scale cybersecurity dataset containing 100,000 IoT-cloud events and multiple attack categories, including unauthorized access, distributed denial-of-service (DDoS), malware, credential theft, replay attacks, botnet command-and-control activity, data tampering, MQTT flooding, and jamming attacks. Experimental results demonstrate a detection rate of 93.0%, a threat classification accuracy of 91.8%, and an average trust score of 0.716 across 500 evaluated attack events. Furthermore, the blockchain logging component successfully maintained ledger integrity throughout all experiments, providing tamper-resistant storage of incident evidence.

The proposed framework demonstrates that the combination of multi-agent artificial intelligence, explainable threat intelligence, trust-aware automation, and blockchain-backed auditability can significantly enhance the effectiveness and reliability of cyber defense operations in IoT-cloud environments. The results indicate the potential of the proposed architecture as a practical foundation for next-generation autonomous cybersecurity systems.
