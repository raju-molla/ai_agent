# V. Results and Discussion

## A. Detection Agent Performance

The first stage of the proposed framework evaluates the ability of the Detection Agent to distinguish malicious events from normal system behavior. Using temporally separated training and testing data, the Random Forest-based Detection Agent achieved an overall classification accuracy of 98.54% and an F1-score of 95.90%.

The confusion matrix analysis demonstrated that the model successfully identified the vast majority of attack events while maintaining a very low false-positive rate. The achieved precision of 98.75% indicates that nearly all events classified as attacks corresponded to genuine malicious activities. Similarly, the recall value of 93.21% confirms that the model was capable of detecting the majority of attack instances present within the evaluation dataset.

These results suggest that operational telemetry features, network communication indicators, and device-level measurements contain sufficient discriminatory information for accurate attack detection without relying on explicit attack labels or highly correlated security indicators. The use of temporally separated evaluation further strengthens the validity of the obtained results by reducing the likelihood of information leakage and overfitting.

Feature importance analysis revealed that network latency, jitter, packet loss, uplink traffic volume, and edge-to-cloud communication delay were among the most influential variables contributing to attack detection. These observations are consistent with real-world attack behavior, where malicious activities frequently introduce abnormal communication patterns and network performance degradation.

## B. Threat Analysis Agent Performance

The Threat Analysis Agent was evaluated on malicious events to assess its ability to identify specific attack categories. Experimental results demonstrated a threat classification accuracy of 91.8%, indicating strong performance across multiple attack classes.

The hierarchical classification strategy proved effective in distinguishing between attack families before performing fine-grained attack identification. This approach reduced classification ambiguity and enabled the framework to capture both high-level threat semantics and specific attack characteristics.

The strongest classification performance was observed for attack categories exhibiting distinct behavioral signatures, such as unauthorized access, malware activity, and data tampering. More challenging categories included attacks with partially overlapping communication patterns, particularly network flooding attacks such as DDoS and MQTT flooding. Nevertheless, the model maintained satisfactory classification performance across all evaluated attack classes.

The achieved accuracy demonstrates the feasibility of integrating hierarchical threat intelligence mechanisms into autonomous cybersecurity systems and highlights the effectiveness of the selected feature set for attack categorization.

## C. Explainability Analysis

A key objective of the proposed framework is to improve transparency in AI-driven cybersecurity operations. The Explainability Agent generated feature-based explanations for every analyzed attack event by identifying the most influential variables contributing to the classification decision.

For example, incidents classified as unauthorized access attacks were primarily associated with elevated port scan scores, increased credential risk indicators, abnormal payload entropy values, and network communication anomalies. These explanations provide meaningful insights into the reasoning process of the underlying machine learning models and enable analysts to validate system-generated conclusions.

The incorporation of explainable threat intelligence addresses a major limitation of conventional black-box cybersecurity systems and supports analyst trust, accountability, and forensic investigation processes.

## D. Trust Manager Evaluation

The Trust Manager was evaluated using confidence values generated by both the Detection Agent and Threat Analysis Agent. Across the evaluated events, the framework achieved an average trust score of 0.716.

The trust evaluation mechanism enabled the framework to categorize security decisions into different operational levels. Table II summarizes the distribution of trust decisions observed during the system evaluation.

### Table II. Trust Decision Distribution

| Decision Category | Number of Events |
| ----------------- | ---------------- |
| Human Review      | 330              |
| Auto Response     | 122              |
| Escalate          | 13               |
| Missed Detection  | 35               |

The results indicate that the majority of incidents were assigned to the human-review category, reflecting the conservative nature of the trust evaluation mechanism. This behavior is desirable in cybersecurity environments because it reduces the likelihood of incorrect autonomous actions while still enabling automation for highly confident incidents.

The trust-aware decision process therefore provides an additional safety layer that balances operational efficiency and risk management.

## E. Blockchain Logger Evaluation

The Blockchain Logger was evaluated to determine its ability to preserve incident evidence in an immutable and verifiable manner.

Throughout the evaluation process, every incident report generated by the framework was successfully recorded within the blockchain ledger. Integrity verification procedures confirmed that all blocks satisfied hash-chain consistency requirements, resulting in a ledger validation rate of 100%.

No integrity violations or chain inconsistencies were observed during experimentation. These findings demonstrate that the proposed blockchain-based logging mechanism can provide reliable forensic traceability and tamper-resistant evidence preservation for cybersecurity operations.

The integration of blockchain technology therefore extends the framework beyond conventional intrusion detection systems by introducing verifiable incident accountability and long-term evidence integrity.

## F. End-to-End Framework Evaluation

To evaluate the effectiveness of the complete architecture, 500 attack events were processed through the entire multi-agent pipeline. The evaluation incorporated attack detection, threat analysis, explanation generation, response recommendation, trust assessment, and blockchain logging.

The overall system achieved a detection rate of 93.0% and a threat classification accuracy of 91.8%. The average detection confidence was 0.802, while the average attack classification confidence reached 0.678. The resulting average trust score across all evaluated incidents was 0.716.

Furthermore, the complete framework maintained blockchain integrity throughout all experiments and achieved an average processing time of 0.338 seconds per event. These results indicate that the proposed architecture is capable of generating comprehensive security intelligence and response recommendations within practical operational time constraints.

## G. Discussion

The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed framework successfully integrates multiple cybersecurity capabilities within a unified architecture. Unlike traditional intrusion detection systems that focus solely on attack identification, the proposed framework provides a complete cyber defense workflow encompassing detection, classification, explanation, response generation, trust evaluation, and forensic logging.

The obtained results further indicate that trust-aware decision support can enhance the safety of autonomous cybersecurity operations by reducing the risk of executing unreliable recommendations. Similarly, the Explainability Agent improves transparency and facilitates analyst understanding of AI-generated decisions.

From a forensic perspective, the Blockchain Logger provides a significant advantage over conventional logging mechanisms by ensuring tamper-resistant evidence preservation and verifiable audit trails. Consequently, the proposed framework addresses multiple limitations of existing cybersecurity architectures while maintaining strong detection and classification performance.

Overall, the results confirm that the combination of multi-agent artificial intelligence, explainable threat intelligence, trust-aware automation, and blockchain-based accountability constitutes a promising approach for next-generation IoT-cloud cyber defense systems.
